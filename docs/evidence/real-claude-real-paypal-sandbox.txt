ANSWER:
Two methods suit small apartments: a sealed bokashi bucket or a worm bin.

**Choose a method.** In a four-week bench test, the sealed bokashi bucket had the lowest odor every week. The worm bin needed less hands-on time once established and produced finished castings. Both fit under a standard sink [1].

**Worm bin basics.** One pound of red wigglers processes about half a pound of scraps a day, which is roughly what one person generates. A pound of worms in a 10-gallon bin is a good start. Keep citrus, onion, garlic, meat, dairy and oily food out. Bury scraps under bedding, and feed only when the last meal is mostly gone. Overfeeding causes nearly every smell and fruit-fly outbreak [2].

**Prevent smells.** Odor usually comes from too much moisture, too little air or too many greens. Mix in dry browns such as shredded paper or cardboard. Freeze scraps until you're ready to add them, and keep a tight lid on anything anaerobic [3].

**Daily habits.** Chop scraps smaller than a thumbnail so they break down faster. Keep a small counter container and empty it daily so scraps don't sit and smell [4].
